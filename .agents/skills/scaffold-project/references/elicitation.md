# BA elicitation guide

Use this guide after hearing the user's idea. It is a set of decision lenses, not a questionnaire to recite.

## Choose questions by information value

First summarize what the user has already established. For each gap, ask whether its answer could materially change the brief's problem, users, intended outcome, MVP boundary, constraint, or credibility. Prioritize the highest-impact gaps. Ask roughly three to five related questions in a round when several matter; ask fewer when one decision unlocks the rest. Give concise suggested options when they help, while leaving room for the user's own answer.

## Lenses

- **Problem and present situation:** What happens today, what is painful or costly, and why is this worth solving now? A proposed feature does not itself establish the problem.
- **People and setting:** Who experiences the problem, who would use the product, and in what situation? Distinguish a buyer, operator, data provider, and end user when the idea implies different actors.
- **Task and change:** What would the user do from start to finish, what information would they provide or receive, and what observable result would make the task successful?
- **MVP boundary:** Which single use case must work first, what may be deferred, and what would make a first version useful enough to show or test?
- **Evidence and impact:** What is known from experience or supplied material? If the idea claims environmental impact, what causal mechanism is expected and what data could later test it? Do not assume a benefit or invent a baseline.
- **Constraints:** Ask about a deadline, audience, accessibility, privacy, legal, integration, or operating constraint only when the idea makes it relevant. Technical choices come later unless the user has already fixed them.

For this event, the kickoff PDF supplies a deadline and pitch format. After hearing the idea, ask whether the team intends to enter Impact and what can be demonstrated by that deadline if those choices are still unclear. Climate action is an example within Impact, not a predetermined product requirement.

## Adaptation and stopping

Do not repeat an answered question. If an answer is uncertain, record the uncertainty and ask about its consequence rather than forcing a guess. If the user offers a solution, ask what problem it solves before specifying it. If two answers conflict, show the conflict plainly and ask for a decision. Stop eliciting when the four anchors—problem, affected people, intended change, and candidate MVP boundary—are coherent enough for a reviewable brief. Leave secondary unknowns in the brief's Open questions instead of prolonging discovery.

Record each answer verbatim with an ID in the intake. In the brief, cite the answer ID for every material business rule. Keep confirmed facts, proposed hypotheses, and choices made by the user distinct.

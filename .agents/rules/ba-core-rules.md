# BA discovery rules

## Evidence

- Use the user's current description, their later answers, and sources they explicitly supply. Cite each material business rule to the request, an intake answer, or an exact source path.
- Preserve the original request and important quoted wording verbatim, including French. Summaries may be in English but cannot change its meaning.
- Label a possible solution, target, user group, environmental benefit, or measurement method as a **Hypothesis** until the user confirms it or a supplied source supports it.
- Do not infer a technology stack, architecture, regulation, dataset, emission factor, or precise impact figure.

## Conversation

- Begin with an open invitation if the user has not described the idea. Do not ask a prewritten list before hearing it.
- After each answer, update the known facts, assumptions, and gaps. Ask at most a few high-value questions in one round; combine related issues and explain any question whose purpose is not obvious.
- Adapt to the user's answer. Do not repeat answered questions or force every field of the template to be filled.
- Ask about the current situation and pain point before features; ask about people and their task before screens or technology. Probe a proposed environmental benefit for its mechanism and available evidence, without assuming that benefit exists.
- If the user cannot answer, record `Not provided.` or a hypothesis and continue where possible.

## Brief quality

- Create an intake record as soon as an idea exists and keep adding exact answers as discovery proceeds.
- Draft a brief when there is enough to state a credible problem, at least one affected user or stakeholder, the intended change, and a candidate MVP boundary. If one is unknown, show the gap in the brief.
- Keep the brief as living context without an approval state. Make a material contradiction or unanswered decision visible before using the affected boundary in a PRD. Optional metrics, branding, technical choices, and unsupported impact estimates do not alone prevent review.
- Do not turn the brief into a PRD, architecture decision, design spec, or implementation plan.
